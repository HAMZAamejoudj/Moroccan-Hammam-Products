# Moroccan Hammam Treatment Steps: A Protocol Your Spa Can Use Tomorrow

Many spas sell a "Moroccan hammam" that is really a body scrub in a warm room. Clients who have been to Morocco notice. The real ritual is simple, but the order, the timing and the pressure matter. Here is the protocol we share with spa clients, with product amounts so you can cost it.

## Before you start: room and preparation

- **Heat and humidity:** a steam room at about 40 to 45°C with high humidity. A wet room with a heated marble bench also works if you do not have steam.
- **Water:** two buckets or basins, one warm, one cooler, and a bowl (tassa) for pouring.
- **Products per client:** black soap [40 to 60 g], ghassoul [50 to 80 g] mixed with rose water, kessa glove (one per client, or sanitized between clients per your local rules), argan oil [10 to 15 ml].
- **Duration:** 45 minutes for a classic hammam, 60 to 75 minutes with a wrap and massage.

## The 7 steps

### 1. Steam and warm water (10 minutes)
The client rests in the heat. Pour warm water over the body a few times. Skin must be fully softened before soap; rushing this step is the most common reason the kessa "does not work".

### 2. Black soap application (2 minutes)
Spread a thin, even layer of black soap over the body, avoiding the eyes. No rubbing needed. It is a paste, not a foam.

### 3. Rest (5 to 10 minutes)
The client rests with the soap on. This is when the soap softens dead skin. Use the time to offer water or mint tea.

### 4. Rinse
Rinse thoroughly with warm water. Leftover soap makes the glove slide instead of grip.

### 5. Kessa exfoliation (10 to 15 minutes)
With the glove on, use long, firm strokes in one direction, section by section: back, arms, legs, front. Lighter pressure on the chest, inner arms and neck. Rinse the glove often. Show the client the rolled skin if they want to see it; most do.

### 6. Ghassoul wrap (10 minutes)
Apply the ghassoul paste over the body (and face, if wanted). Leave for 5 to 10 minutes, keeping it moist; do not let it dry and crack. Rinse with warm water, then a final cooler pour.

### 7. Rose water and argan oil
Mist rose water on the face. In the relaxation room, massage argan oil into the skin, or offer a full massage as an upgrade.

## Mistakes that ruin the experience

- **Not enough heat.** Cold skin will not exfoliate; the client feels scratched, not cleaned.
- **Scented, foaming "black soap".** Clients who know the real thing will notice immediately.
- **Too much pressure on sensitive areas.** Train therapists on a colleague first.
- **Skipping the rest after the soap.** It is the step that makes the kessa work.
- **Letting clay dry on the skin.** It pulls and irritates.

## Who should not do a hammam

Ask on your intake form about pregnancy, heart conditions, high blood pressure, recent sunburn, broken skin, skin conditions and recent waxing or peeling. When in doubt, suggest a gentler version or ask the client to check with their doctor.

## Menu ideas

- **Classic Hammam (45 min):** steps 1 to 5, rinse, oil.
- **Royal Hammam (75 min):** all 7 steps plus a 20-minute argan massage.
- **Bride's Hammam:** add a nila mask for the face and rose petals in the water.
- **Take-home kit:** black soap, kessa and ghassoul, sold at reception after the treatment.

## FAQ

**Can I offer a hammam without a steam room?**
Yes, in a warm wet room with a heated bench and lots of warm water, but extend step 1.

**Should each client get a new kessa?**
Many spas give the glove to the client as part of the price; it doubles as a souvenir and solves hygiene questions.

**How long should therapist training take?**
One practical day is usually enough for the protocol; pressure control takes a few weeks of practice.
