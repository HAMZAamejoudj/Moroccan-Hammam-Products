# How to Launch a Private Label Hammam Kit (Without Overspending)

A hammam kit is one of the easiest private label products to launch. It has a built-in story, it photographs well, and it makes a natural gift. It is also easy to overcomplicate. This guide walks through the decisions in the order you will actually face them.

## Step 1: Pick the kit size

| Kit | Contents | Positioning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starter (3 pieces) | Black soap, kessa, ghassoul | Entry price, best first launch |
| Classic (4 pieces) | + argan oil | Mid-range gift |
| Ritual (6 pieces) | + rose water, bath towel or nila mask | Premium, spa retail, hotels |

Our advice: launch with the starter or classic kit. You learn what sells before tying up cash in six SKUs.

## Step 2: Choose packaging that fits your channel

- **Online shop:** sturdy box with inserts; glass jars need protection in the post.
- **Spa reception:** cotton drawstring bag or open basket, so clients see and touch the products.
- **Retail shelf:** printed box with a window or clear front, ingredient list and how-to on the back.
- **Hotels and gifts:** small sizes [50 to 100 g], light packaging.

Think about weight early. A 6-piece kit in glass can weigh over a kilo, which changes your shipping cost per unit.

## Step 3: Write the label and instructions

Each product needs its own label with: product name, INCI ingredients, net quantity, batch number, period after opening, your company details and (in the EU) your Responsible Person. The kit box should carry a short ritual: warm skin, soap, rest, kessa, clay, oil. Customers who follow the steps get results; customers who do not, complain.

## Step 4: Do the pricing math

A simple way to start:

1. Add your landed cost per kit (products + packaging + shipping + duties).
2. Multiply by about [2] for your wholesale price to shops.
3. Multiply the wholesale price by about [2 to 2.5] for the retail price.

If the retail price looks too high for your market, reduce the kit size or switch packaging before cutting product quality.

## Step 5: Samples and approval

Order a full sample kit with your labels before production. Open it like a customer: does it look premium, does the soap leak, is the instruction clear? Change things now; changes after production are expensive.

## Step 6: Production and shipping

Typical timeline [to confirm with real lead times]:

1. Week 1 to 2: samples and packaging choice.
2. Week 2 to 3: label design and proof approval.
3. Week 3 to 5: production, filling, kitting.
4. Week 6: documents and dispatch.

## Launch mistakes we see often

- Too many scent variants at launch.
- Beautiful packaging that crushes in transit.
- No instructions in the kit.
- Medical claims on the box ("detox", "treats").
- Forgetting to order spare labels and boxes for reorders.

## Selling tips

- Show the ritual in a short video; the kessa moment sells the kit.
- Bundle for occasions: bridal, Ramadan and Eid, Christmas, Mother's Day.
- In spas, sell the kit right after the treatment, when the client still feels the result.
