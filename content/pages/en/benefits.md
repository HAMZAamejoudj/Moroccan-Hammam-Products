# What Each Hammam Product Does, and Why It Works Together

A Moroccan hammam is a sequence, not a single product. Steam opens the skin, black soap softens it, the kessa removes what is ready to come off, clay cleans, and oil restores. Each step prepares the next. That is why the full ritual gives a result no single product can.

## [products] Benefits of each product

### [black-soap] Moroccan black soap
- Softens rough skin before exfoliation, so the kessa works with less pressure.
- Rich in olive oil, so it cleans without the tight feeling of standard soaps.
- Thick paste, no foam: easy to spread over the whole body in one minute.

### [kessa] Kessa glove
- Mechanical exfoliation with no grains or added actives.
- Visible result in one session: smoother, brighter-looking skin.
- Reusable for months if washed and dried properly.

### [ghassoul] Ghassoul clay
- Naturally rich in minerals such as magnesium and silica.
- Absorbs excess oil and leaves skin feeling clean and soft.
- Works on body, face and hair (as a no-foam hair wash).

### [argan-oil] Argan oil
- High in vitamin E and fatty acids that help skin hold moisture.
- Light texture, absorbs fast, good for post-hammam massage.
- Also used on hair ends and nails.

### [rose-water] Rose water
- Gentle toner after cleansing.
- Fresh scent that clients associate with Morocco.

### [nila-aker] Nila powder and aker fassi
- Nila (indigo plant powder) is used in masks for a more even-looking, radiant complexion.
- Aker fassi (poppy petals) gives a natural tint for lips and cheeks.

## [ritual] The ritual, step by step

1. Steam and warm water (about 10 minutes) until the skin is fully softened.
2. Spread a thin layer of black soap over the body; no rubbing needed.
3. Rest with the soap on for 5 to 10 minutes while the paste softens dead skin.
4. Rinse thoroughly, then exfoliate with the kessa in long, firm strokes section by section.
5. Apply ghassoul paste over the body, keep it moist, then rinse with warm and cooler water.
6. Mist rose water on the face and massage argan oil into the skin to finish.

## [business] Benefits for your business

- **Low cost per treatment, high price on the menu.** One full-body hammam uses roughly 40 to 60 g of black soap and 50 to 80 g of ghassoul. At wholesale prices, product cost is a small part of a treatment that sells for €40 to €90 in Europe.
- **A signature experience.** Clients book it again because the result is visible and the ritual feels authentic.
- **Retail add-on.** Clients who enjoyed the treatment buy the kit to take home. Spas often sell about 1 kit for every 4 to 6 treatments.
- **Easy to train.** Therapists learn the six steps in one session.

## [storage] How to store and use them

- Black soap: closed jar, away from heat. Use within [24] months.
- Ghassoul powder: dry place; mix only the amount you need.
- Oils: dark bottles, out of sunlight; use within [12 to 18] months of opening.
- Kessa: rinse, wring and dry flat after each use. Replace when the fabric loses grip.

## [faq] FAQ

**Is the Moroccan hammam suitable for sensitive skin?**
Yes, with lighter kessa pressure and a shorter soap time. Clients with broken skin, sunburn or a skin condition should check with a doctor first.

**How often should a client do a hammam?**
Once a week to once every two weeks is typical in Morocco. More often can irritate the skin.

**Can black soap be used on the face?**
Yes, briefly and rinsed well. Many clients prefer ghassoul for the face.

**What is the difference between ghassoul and rhassoul?**
None. It is the same clay; the name is spelled differently when transliterated from Arabic.
